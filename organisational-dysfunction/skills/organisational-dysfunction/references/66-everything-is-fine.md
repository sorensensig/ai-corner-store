# Everything is fine

*Dysfunction `#66` in Trond Hjorteland's ["Organisational Dysfunction of the Day"](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/trondhjort_opensystemstheory-sociotechnical-orgdesign-activity-7491010390735257600-qiVa) series — synthesised through open sociotechnical systems theory; paraphrased, not quoted.*

## How it shows up

- Every status is green until the day the thing fails, and then everyone says they saw it coming.
- Watermelon reporting: green on the outside, red on the inside — the RAG status is a negotiation, not a measurement.
- Concerns were written down, raised, escalated — and somehow never arrived anywhere that could act on them.
- The higher you go, the more confident the account of the work becomes and the less it resembles the work.
- After the failure, an investigation finds the warnings sitting in a report from a year ago that nobody disputes and nobody read.

## The sociotechnical diagnosis

Emery and Emery described the organisation as a **filter and amplifier system**: information does not travel up a hierarchy unchanged, it travels through people whose position determines what they can afford to pass on. In a DP1 structure the two ends of that channel distort in the same direction. Below, a subordinate's standing depends on the judgement of the person above, so bad news is softened, hedged, or held one more sprint in the hope it resolves itself. Above, a manager who cannot act on a problem — no authority over the constraint, no slack to absorb it — has every reason not to receive it, because receiving it makes them accountable for something they cannot fix. Each hop attenuates the signal a little and amplifies the reassurance a little.

Compound that over four or five levels and you get the characteristic result: the further you are from the work, the more confident you are, and the further you are from reality. This is not a few people lying. It is an entire channel doing exactly what its design rewards, which is why the distortion is so consistent — everyone's local incentive points the same way, so the errors accumulate rather than cancel.

It also explains why the usual countermeasures do nothing. A dashboard, a governance forum, a mandated escalation path or an "open door policy" all add another hop to the same filtered channel; they change the container, not the gradient. Nor is this a candour problem to be solved with a values poster: people are reading a structure in which delivering bad news upward is personally costly and organisationally useless, and responding sensibly. Variance is being detected at the point of work and then travelling — badly — to a level that must decide about it, which is the DP1 failure mode in its purest form.

## What to do

**The real fix is structural — stop making information travel up to be acted on, and the filter has nothing to act on.**
- Move to DP2: give the people who see the variance the authority and resources to correct it where it appears, so most bad news never needs to become a report at all.
- Make accountability for outcomes genuinely shared across the group rather than held individually by whoever reports upward — nobody has a private status to protect if the status is collective.
- Where information must cross a boundary, let it travel between peers doing the work rather than up and back down through role holders whose standing depends on the message.
- Remove the ways bad news is punished, including the polite ones: reforecasting, "why wasn't this flagged earlier", and asking for a recovery plan in the same meeting the problem is first named.

**If you can't change the structure yet:**
- Go and look. Talk to the people doing the work rather than reading the roll-up — the accurate account exists, it just isn't in the report.
- Treat an unbroken run of green as a signal about the reporting channel, not about the work. Real work has variance; a status that never varies is measuring something else.
- When you get bad news, respond in a way that makes the next piece cheaper to deliver — visible relief, not visible cost. This buys you a local exception, not a fixed system, and it lasts exactly as long as you do.
- Say plainly that the filter is structural. It defuses the blame that lands on whoever "should have escalated", which is otherwise the standard post-mortem finding and the one that guarantees a repeat.
