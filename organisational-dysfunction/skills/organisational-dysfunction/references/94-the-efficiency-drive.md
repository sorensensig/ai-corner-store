# The efficiency drive

*Dysfunction `#94` in Trond Hjorteland's ["Organisational Dysfunction of the Day"](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/trondhjort_opensystemstheory-sociotechnical-orgdesign-ugcPost-7501900867789504513-ZLtQ/) series — synthesised through open sociotechnical systems theory; paraphrased, not quoted.*

## How it shows up

- A restructuring is announced — flatter, leaner, "more agile" — and a redundancy list is drawn up on criteria that sound fair: visible contribution, measurable output.
- The cut lands on the tech lead who spent half their week unblocking others, the coordinator who kept three teams in step, the senior who remembered why the system is built the way it is. The people left are busy, productive and easy to defend.
- For a quarter nothing seems to break. Then onboarding takes twice as long, cross-team work stalls at every handoff, and the same questions get asked in five channels with nobody able to answer.
- Knowledge nobody had catalogued turns out to have lived in a handful of heads that are now elsewhere.
- The programme reports its savings as delivered. The organisation is measurably less capable, and no line on any dashboard shows why.

## The sociotechnical diagnosis

The selection criteria were not neutral; they were the design principle speaking. **DP1 — redundancy of parts** — builds the organisation from interchangeable fragments, each with a countable output, integrated by someone above. Its measurement regime follows: value is what an individual visibly produces — tickets closed, hours billed, features shipped. Anything whose contribution belongs to **the whole rather than a part** has no column to appear in. Knowledge transfer, translation between teams, informal coordination and the memory of past decisions are not low-value work in this frame; they are *unmeasurable* work, and an efficiency drive run on DP1 metrics reads unmeasurable as optional.

So the cut is systematic, not unlucky. OST has always distinguished the **formal organisation** on the chart from the **working organisation** that makes the chart function — the lateral network through which variances actually get handled near where they arise. In DP1 that network is off the books by construction, because coordination officially sits one level up. Downsizing by visible output removes the people who *were* that network, while every box on the chart survives intact. It is **suboptimisation of the whole** carried out deliberately: each retained part is more efficient, and the system they make up does less.

The Parkinson corollary completes it. Bureaucracies grow by adding subordinates and titled roles; when they shrink, they shed what has no title first. Connective tissue has none, so it goes before anything that looks like a job. And what follows is predictable — the coordination gap gets read as a management gap, and the next reorg adds a layer to supply what the cut removed.

## What to do

**The real fix is structural — make the whole-system work a designed function of teams, so it is visible, shared and measured where it happens.**
- Measure the group's whole task, not individual fragments. When a team owns an end-to-end outcome, onboarding, coordination and knowledge-holding count toward the result instead of competing with it.
- Design redundancy of functions into teams — multi-skilling and deliberately shared coordination — so the working organisation is not concentrated in three people a spreadsheet can find and remove.
- Before any restructuring, map the working organisation, not just the chart: who gets asked, who translates, who carries context across boundaries. Let the people doing the work draw it, because nobody above them can.
- If costs must come down, let teams redesign their own work to find the savings (a Participative Design Workshop is built for this) rather than cutting from a list drawn where the connective work is invisible.

**If you can't change the structure yet:**
- Challenge the criteria before the list is final, not the names on it. "Visible output" is a question you can raise at almost any altitude: *what does this measure miss, and who does that work today?*
- If the cut has happened, name what stopped rather than who left — onboarding time, stalled dependencies, questions nobody can answer — and put numbers on it. That turns an invisible loss into a cost the same regime can see.
- Spread the remaining connective work deliberately now: pair on it, write down the context, rotate who holds the cross-team relationships. Don't let it pool in whoever is left who happens to care.
- If you do this work yourself, make it legible in the terms the organisation counts. Its invisibility is a property of the measurement regime, not evidence that the work is worthless.
